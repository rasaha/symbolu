# B1.11 — Blind Replication Challenge — PREREGISTRATION / DESIGN REVIEW (docs-only)

**Docs-only design review. No harness, no code, no freeze, no run commands, no frozen artifact modified. B1.10 is
untouched and not re-run.** Purpose: stress-test whether the B1.10 positive descriptive pilot (happy, peace, love)
is likely to replicate on **completely different words**, or whether it depended on unusually favorable word
selection. Resonance / phonetic-fidelity refinement only — **no `GENUTILITY_*`, no `ONTOLOGICAL_SIGNAL`, no
semantic-truth / ontology / Sanskrit-privilege claim.** B1.4b′ remains `NULL_RETURN_BOTTOM`; original B1.4b
blocked; Track B blocked. **Structure, not validated meaning.**

Method = exactly B1.10: same word in two contexts (binding / other-conditioned vs liberating / self-grounded);
two **context-invariant** packets fixed by the word's varṇas (read-only from the frozen v3 table); judge rates
source-condition fit 0–6; primary statistic `context_pole_margin = (fit(Pb|Cb)−fit(Pl|Cb)) + (fit(Pl|Cl)−fit(Pb|Cl))`.

**Excluded (must not reuse):** happy, peace, love, longing, devotion.

**Provenance of packet notes below.** Varṇa sequences and packet facets were obtained by a **read-only** lookup of
the frozen v3 table via `varna_bridge_active` (no file modified). Facets are quoted (trimmed). Contexts below are
**illustrative drafts for review only** — see Failure Mode F7: in the real scale-up they must be authored **blind
to the packets** and pre-registered.

## 1. Candidate pool (12 words; emotional / cognitive / relational mix)

For each: varṇa sequence, a binding context, a liberating context, why each context is the expected
source-condition, and a confidence class judged against the **actual** v3 packet.

### 1. pride — `pa, ra, da` — emotional — **HIGH**
- **Binding ctx:** *"His pride fed on looking down at those beneath him, and curdled to contempt when outshone."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her pride was a quiet self-respect that needed no one's inferiority to stand."*
- **Why:** binding = comparative, contemptuous, other-referenced esteem; liberating = self-grounded dignity.
- **Packet fit:** binding `[pa]` ghṛṇā (revulsion/contempt) fits contempt-pride sharply; liberating `[pa]` the
  upward turn / goodwill fits dignified pride. `[ra]` defeat↔vitality and `[da]` peevishness↔forbearance align.
  **Clean discriminator on `[pa]`.**

### 2. courage — `ka, ra, ga` — cognitive/volitional — **HIGH**
- **Binding ctx:** *"His courage was bravado to be seen, driven by dread of looking weak in front of the others."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her courage rose quietly from within, acting rightly whether or not anyone was watching."*
- **Why:** binding = fear/approval-driven display; liberating = self-possessed resolve.
- **Packet fit:** liberating `[ra]` prāṇaśakti (life-force/resolve) + `[ga]` will-force fit courage's energy
  strongly; binding `[ka]` grasping-hope + `[ra]` defeat + `[ga]` restless-striving fit bravado/fear-driven action.

### 3. control — `ka, na, tta, ra, la` — relational — **HIGH** (verbose caveat)
- **Binding ctx:** *"His control gripped every detail of her life, tightening whenever she slipped from his hold."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her control was calm self-mastery: she governed her own reactions and let others be free."*
- **Why:** binding = domination over others; liberating = self-governance that releases others.
- **Packet fit:** binding `[ka]` grasping + `[na]` moha/fixation + `[la]` kruratā (cruelty) fit domineering control
  very well; liberating side (hope-without-attachment, de-fascination, compassion) fits self-mastery. **Caveat:**
  5 facets (verbose); `[tta]` (measured-vs-garrulous speech) is off-axis for control.

### 4. patience — `pa, ta, na, ka` — cognitive/temperamental — **HIGH**
- **Binding ctx:** *"His patience was grudging, a resentful waiting that seethed under a still surface."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her patience was alert and willing, resting easily in the pace of things."*
- **Why:** binding = suppressed, inert, resentful endurance; liberating = awake equanimous forbearance.
- **Packet fit:** binding `[ta]` jāḍya (inertia/torpor) fits dull grudging waiting; liberating `[ta]` cessation of
  torpor / awakening fits alert patience. `[pa]` revulsion↔upward-turn adds a second clean axis. **Strong.**

### 5. freedom — `ra, da, ma` — abstract/relational — **HIGH**
- **Binding ctx:** *"His freedom was license: he indulged every impulse until nothing held together."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her freedom was inner, self-possessed, needing no escape and breaking nothing."*
- **Why:** binding = unrestrained license/dissipation; liberating = grounded inner freedom.
- **Packet fit:** binding `[ma]` praśraya/praṇāśa (indulgence to dissolution) fits license precisely; liberating
  `[ma]` disciplined containment fits true freedom. **Clean discriminator on `[ma]`.** `[ra]`,`[da]` support.

### 6. doubt — `da, ba, ta` — cognitive — **HIGH**
- **Binding ctx:** *"His doubt corroded everything, dismissing worth and sinking him into dull paralysis."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her doubt was honest inquiry that weighed things fairly and woke her mind up."*
- **Why:** binding = corrosive, dismissive, paralyzing self-doubt; liberating = healthy discerning skepticism.
- **Packet fit:** binding `[ba]` avajñā (neglect of worth) + `[ta]` jāḍya (inertia) fit corrosive doubt; liberating
  `[ba]` regard-for-worth + `[ta]` awakening fit inquiring doubt. **Caveat:** `[da]` peevishness is a soft fit.

### 7. curiosity — `ka, ra, sa, ta, ya` — cognitive — **MEDIUM** (verbose)
- **Binding ctx:** *"His curiosity was a grasping, prying hunger to know what wasn't his to know."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her curiosity was open and unhurried, delighting in things without needing to possess them."*
- **Why:** binding = acquisitive/prying; liberating = open inquiry.
- **Packet fit:** `[ka]` grasp↔non-grasp + `[ta]` inertia↔awakening align; but `[sa]` clung-clarity and `[ya]`
  self-doubt are weaker for curiosity; 5 facets (verbose). Usable but not clean.

### 8. gratitude — `ga, ra, ta, ta, da` — emotional — **MEDIUM**
- **Binding ctx:** *"His gratitude was a transaction, a debt he resented owing and expected repaid."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her gratitude welled up freely, asking nothing back and lightening her whole day."*
- **Why:** binding = indebted/transactional; liberating = freely-arising thankfulness.
- **Packet fit:** liberating facets (will-force, vitality, awakening, forbearance) are decent; but binding facets
  (`[ga]` restless-striving, `[ta]` inertia, `[da]` peevishness) **do not name transactional obligation** — the
  binding side is off-axis. Asymmetric.

### 9. ambition — `ma, ba, ta, na` — cognitive/volitional — **MEDIUM**
- **Binding ctx:** *"His ambition was a fixation on winning, trampling worth and never satisfied."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her ambition was a measured aspiration, held lightly and aimed beyond herself."*
- **Why:** binding = ruthless fixated striving; liberating = purposeful aspiration held loosely.
- **Packet fit:** binding `[na]` moha (fixation) + `[ma]` excess fit ruthless ambition; `[ba]` neglect↔regard
  adds an axis. **Caveat:** `[ta]` inertia is a poor fit (ambition is not inert).

### 10. loyalty — `la, ta, ya` — relational — **MEDIUM** (coarse binding)
- **Binding ctx:** *"His loyalty was fearful and blind, clinging to the group even as it did harm."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her loyalty was freely chosen, steady and clear-eyed, trusting without servility."*
- **Why:** binding = coerced/fearful in-group loyalty; liberating = freely-chosen fidelity.
- **Packet fit:** liberating `[ya]` self-efficacy + `[ta]` awakening + `[la]` compassion fit chosen loyalty; but
  binding `[la]` = kruratā (cruelty) is a **coarse** fit (same soft spot as love's `[la]`).

### 11. trust — `tta, ra, sa, ta` — relational — **MEDIUM / LOW**
- **Binding ctx:** *"His trust was naive dependence, clung to blindly until it dulled his judgment."*
- **Liberating ctx:** *"Her trust was grounded and clear, freely given and open-eyed."*
- **Why:** binding = blind/naive dependence; liberating = grounded faith.
- **Packet fit:** **weak.** The lead facet `[tta]` is about *speech* (garrulous overstatement ↔ measured speech) —
  **off-axis for trust**; `[ta]` inertia is a soft fit. Only `[ra]`,`[sa]` partly align. Marginal.

### 12. grief — `ga, ra` — emotional — **REJECT**
- **Binding ctx:** *"His grief hardened into bitter, restless replaying of the loss."*
- **Liberating ctx (forced):** *"Her grief opened into quiet vitality and acceptance."*
- **Why reject:** grief is **intrinsically loss-toned** — an honest *liberating* grief context is a stretch; only
  2 varṇas (thin); `[ga]` restless-striving is off-axis for grief. Rejected as a single-pole, thin item.

## 2. Conservative rejections (screened OUT — do not use)

- **grief** (above): thin (2 varṇas), off-axis binding facet, no honest liberating context.
- **envy** `na,va,ya`, **greed** `ga,ra,da`: binding fit is excellent, but each is **intrinsically single-pole** —
  you cannot write an honest *liberating* envy/greed context, so a forced one is artificial (Failure Mode F4).
- **fear** `ra`: **single varṇa** (one facet) — too thin; liberating-fear is a stretch.
- **wonder** `va,na,da,ra`: neither packet names wonder's source-condition (rigid-clinging / peevishness on the
  binding side are simply wrong for awe). Weak both sides.
- **shame** `sha,ma`: the packet is about **kāma / desire and indulgence**, not shame — a manner/meaning mismatch;
  also touches the ś/ṣ sibilant-swap caveat. Rejected.
- **hope** `ha,pa`: shares `ha,pa` with B1.10's **happy** — reusing it re-tests the same facets and is **not an
  independent replication** (Failure Mode F5). Rejected for the blind-replication goal.
- **anger** `nga,ra`: `[nga]` = vanity/performance is a poor fit for anger, and a *liberating* anger context is
  strained. Borderline; rejected to stay conservative.
- **forgiveness, surrender**: already frozen B1.9 pole-DiD words — **reuse**, not a fresh replication. Excluded.

## 4. Ranked shortlist — best six

Ranked by cleanliness of the discriminating facet **and** whether the word genuinely admits both contexts:

1. **pride** — `[pa]` contempt (ghṛṇā) ↔ goodwill/upward-turn is the single sharpest flip in the pool; pride is
   naturally both comparative (binding) and self-respecting (liberating). Emotional.
2. **freedom** — `[ma]` indulgence-to-dissolution ↔ disciplined containment maps license vs true freedom exactly;
   a clean one-facet discriminator. Abstract/relational.
3. **patience** — `[ta]` jāḍya (torpor) ↔ awakening-from-torpor distinguishes dull grudging waiting from alert
   forbearance, plus a second axis on `[pa]`. Temperamental/cognitive.
4. **courage** — liberating `[ra]` life-force + `[ga]` will-force vs binding grasping/defeat; strong two-facet
   support and an easy both-context word. Volitional.
5. **control** — binding `[ka]` grasp + `[na]` fixation + `[la]` cruelty fit domination vividly; clear
   self-mastery liberating reading. Relational. (Downgraded slightly for verbosity + one off-axis `[tta]`.)
6. **doubt** — `[ba]` neglect-of-worth ↔ regard and `[ta]` inertia ↔ awakening give two aligned axes for
   corrosive-vs-inquiring doubt. Cognitive. (Ranked 6th for the soft `[da]` facet.)

Mix check: emotional (pride), abstract (freedom), temperamental (patience), volitional (courage), relational
(control), cognitive (doubt) — spread across the three requested families.

## 5. Why each shortlisted word is expected to discriminate cleanly

- **pride / freedom / patience** each have **one facet whose binding and liberating readings are near-opposites**
  and that facet sits squarely on the word's own source-condition (contempt vs dignity; license vs containment;
  torpor vs alertness). A judge reading the sentence should map it to the matching pole without needing pole labels.
- **courage / control** win on **converging multi-facet support** (energy/resolve for courage; grip/fixation for
  control) even though one facet each is slightly off-axis.
- **doubt** has **two independently aligned axes** (`[ba]`, `[ta]`), giving redundancy if one is judged weakly.
- All six are **genuinely bipolar words** — an honest binding *and* liberating context can be written without
  strain (unlike envy/greed/grief), which is the precondition for a fair two-context test.

## 6. Failure modes (explicit)

- **F1 — Valence-tone confound (the biggest threat).** Every v3 **binding** packet is uniformly *negative-toned*
  (attachment / grasping / defeat / cruelty / inertia) and every **liberating** packet uniformly *positive-toned*
  (non-attachment / vitality / dharma / awakening). A judge could score correctly by simply matching a
  negative-valence description to the negative/other-conditioned context and a positive one to the self-grounded
  context — **with no word-specific or varṇa-specific information at all.** The B1.10 +9 margin may be largely this
  effect. A real scale-up **needs a valence-only control** (e.g. generic positive/negative packets *not* derived
  from the word's varṇas) so the varṇa-derived margin can be measured *above* the valence baseline. Until that
  control exists, a positive B1.11 is **not** evidence of anything beyond valence-matching.
- **F2 — Context-cue leakage.** Binding contexts carry overtly negative cues (rival, contempt, domination);
  liberating contexts carry positive cues (quiet, freely, needing nothing). Judges may match packet valence to
  **context** valence rather than to a source-condition. Overlaps with F1; mitigation is valence-balanced context
  writing and the F1 control.
- **F3 — Facet coarseness / off-axis facets.** Several words carry a facet that does not name their actual
  source-condition (love `[la]`=cruelty; trust `[tta]`=speech; gratitude's binding facets; control `[tta]`). A word
  can still "win" on valence despite off-axis facets — masking that the mapping is not really word-appropriate.
- **F4 — Single-pole words.** envy / greed / grief / fear are intrinsically one-pole; a forced opposite context is
  artificial and would inflate or deflate the margin. Screened out here, but a scale-up author must keep screening.
- **F5 — Varṇa-set overlap / non-independence.** Words sharing varṇas share packets (hope↔happy on `ha,pa`;
  courage `ka,ra,ga`, greed `ga,ra,da`, pride `pa,ra,da`, freedom `ra,da,ma` all share `ra`/`da`). "12 different
  words" can collapse to a few distinct facet-sets, so replications are **not** statistically independent. A
  scale-up should measure varṇa-set overlap and cap shared facets.
- **F6 — Judge world-knowledge / Sanskrit recognition.** Judges may recognize the tantric vocabulary (dharma,
  mokṣa, prāṇaśakti) and infer an intended "spiritual = good" framing, biasing toward the liberating packet in
  self-grounded contexts. Mitigation: paraphrase facets into plain English stripped of Sanskrit, and/or use judges
  without that prior.
- **F7 — Experimenter degrees of freedom in context writing.** Whoever writes the two contexts knows the expected
  pole and may unconsciously echo the packet's specific imagery. Mitigation: contexts authored **blind to the
  packets** (by a party who has not seen them) and **pre-registered** before any run; the drafts in §1 are for
  review only and would be re-authored blind.
- **F8 — Reversible / mislabeled expected pole.** Some words flip which pole is "expected" by reading (freedom-as-
  license is the *binding* pole yet colloquially positive; pride can be virtuous or vicious). A mislabeled expected
  pole silently corrupts the margin sign. Mitigation: fix and pre-register each item's expected pole with rationale.
- **F9 — Correlated judges / weak independence.** Two Claude-family judges are correlated; "independent" is
  limited. Genuine replication needs **cross-family** judges (non-Anthropic models) and more of them.
- **F10 — Attribution ceiling (design-level).** Even a clean B1.11 replication would show only **source-condition /
  resonance legibility to judges**. Because each packet is a *bag of constituent-varṇa readings*, the design
  **cannot** attribute any signal to an individual varṇa, cannot establish word-specific mapping, and makes no
  ontology / semantic-truth / Sanskrit-privilege / generation-utility claim.

## 7. Bottom line (design review verdict — no run)

B1.10 **plausibly replicates** on the six shortlisted words *at the descriptive level*, because each has a clean or
converging discriminating facet and admits both contexts. **But** the single most important caveat is **F1**: the
current design cannot separate "varṇa-derived source-condition legibility" from "generic positive/negative valence
matching." Therefore the recommended next step is **not** simply "run B1.11 on six new words," but a
**preregistered scale-up that adds a valence-only control arm** (and, ideally, cross-family judges + blind context
authoring), so any margin can be read *above* the valence baseline. Absent that control, a positive B1.11 would
restate B1.10's bounded claim on new words, not strengthen it. **Structure, not validated meaning.**
