# B1.3 — Initial-Silent-Consonant → Liberating-Polarity Rule (exploratory application)

## The rule

For a word whose spelling begins with a silent consonant (`kn-`, `gn-`, `wr-`, `ps-`/`pn-`, `mn-`, silent
`h-`), prepend that consonant's varṇa at its **liberating** pole, then append the normal spoken-only varṇa
route. Liberating poles used:

| initial silent | varṇa | liberating gloss (frozen) |
|---|---|---|
| K (`kn-`) | `ka` | forward-orientation without attachment (non-attachment / detachment) |
| G (`gn-`) | `ga` | effort that can rest — stillness when action completes |
| W (`wr-`) | `va` | truth-assent purified by discernment / non-binding recognition |
| P (`ps-`,`pn-`) | `pa` | goodwill that dissolves revulsion |
| M (`mn-`) | `ma` | disciplined containment that holds form |
| H (silent `h-`) | `ha` | realized knowing without ownership |

## What this shows (unchanged diagnosis)

Applying the rule does **not** produce broad conformance. The same mechanism explains the pattern: every
liberating pole is a **positive mental/ethical quality** (non-attachment, rest, discernment, goodwill,
egoless knowing), so it "fits" only when the target word **itself denotes a compatible mental quality**
(`honest`), stretches on a few (`knife`=detachment, `write`=discernment, `psalm`=goodwill), **inverts** on
opposite-valence words (`knot`, `wrong`, `pseudo`, `heir`, `gnaw`), and is **unrelated** for the majority
(concrete/neutral words). The apparent hits are explained by **word valence**, not by a silent-consonant→meaning
law. `knife` remains detachment-adjacent-but-not-cutting; it is one word, not a pattern.
