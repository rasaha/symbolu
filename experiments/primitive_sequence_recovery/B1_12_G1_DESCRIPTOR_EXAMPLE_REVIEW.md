## Step 4 — Primary failure categories (a word may have several)

- **affliction-domain mismatch:** ~all 32 (descriptors are affliction/tendency; most referents are not).
- **descriptors too generic / repetition:** pervasive — inherent-`a` → "restless starting without sustaining"
  recurs in nearly every word (parvata, gamana have it 3–4×); descriptors are word-independent per varṇa.
- **polarity mismatch (all-negative binding pole):** every positive-valence word (harṣa, sukha, śānti, satya,
  dharma) receives negative descriptors — sometimes the *opposite* of the referent (śānti/peace ← desire,
  delusion, inertia).
- **coverage gap:** vocalic ṛ has **no** frozen descriptor (gṛha, vṛkṣa) — coverage was 100% only for the
  selected six, not for attested words generally.
- **inventory dominates / order irrelevant:** the descriptor bag drives any impression; order carries no evident
  referential signal here (untested empirically).

## Step 5 — Search for successes (honest)

**No clean successes.** The only above-noise cases are **weak valence/thematic overlaps for negative-affliction
words**: `kāma` (desire) ← `k`="āśā as grasping/clinging hope" is desire-adjacent (the strongest coincidence);
`bhaya` (fear) ← "aviśvāsa/self-doubt", "mūrcchā/stupor"; `krodha/lobha/duḥkha` share negative valence with the
descriptors. These are **valence coincidences, not referent identification** — expected precisely because the
binding descriptors are themselves negative-affliction content, so negative-affliction *referents* partially
overlap in tone. No positive or concrete referent is suggested; several positive referents are actively
contradicted.

## Step 6 — Approximate counts (descriptive only; n = 32)

- **Naturally fitting (a neutral evaluator would pick the referent): 0.**
- **Partially fitting (weak valence/thematic overlap, negative-affliction words only): ~6** (kāma, bhaya,
  krodha, lobha, śoka?, duḥkha).
- **Clearly mismatching: ~26** — including **5 polarity-inverted** positive words (harṣa, sukha, śānti, satya,
  dharma).
- **Effectively impossible for a neutral evaluator at the identity (not valence) level: ~all 32** — no word's
  descriptor bag *identifies* its referent among alternatives.

## Step 7 — Three levels kept separate

- **Observed (literal):** every word's varṇas map to fixed negative affliction/tendency descriptors; inherent-`a`
  repeats "restless starting…" almost everywhere; positive words get negative descriptors; ṛ is uncovered.
- **Interpretive (could a human connect them to the referent?):** essentially never for concrete/positive
  referents; weak valence-only overlap for negative-emotion referents.
- **Theory (does this say Symbol-U is wrong?):** **it does not.** The descriptors are self-consistently in a
  bipolar *tendency/vṛtti* domain (a binding and a liberating pole); this review used only the **binding** pole
  and matched against **ordinary referents** — an instrument choice, not a test of the mapping's intended
  semantics.

## Step 8 — Final assessment

1. **Is the problem primarily the evaluator-facing representation?** **Yes.** Three instrument choices drive the
   failure: (a) candidate domain = ordinary referents while descriptors = psychological tendencies; (b) polarity
   = binding (all-negative) only, contradicting positive referents; (c) generic/repetitive word-independent
   descriptors (inherent-`a`), plus a coverage gap (ṛ) for attested words generally.
2. **Does the evidence show the underlying Symbol-U mappings are in the "wrong" semantic domain?** **No.** The
   mappings are consistently in a tendency domain; "wrong" presumes they *should* be referent descriptors, which
   Symbol-U does not claim. The evidence cannot separate "mapping is wrong" from "mapping may be internally
   meaningful but this referent-matching representation (binding pole vs ordinary referents) is unsuitable" —
   the two remain **confounded**.
3. **Or does it only show the current descriptor representation is unsuitable for ordinary-referent
   identification?** **Yes — this is exactly and only what the examples support.**

**Net:** the examples **narrow** the prior conclusion. They strongly confirm the *instrument/representation* is
unsuitable for referent identification, but they do **not** support the theory-level claim that the Symbol-U
mappings are wrong-domain — that claim should be withdrawn as beyond the evidence. See
`B1_12_G1_EVIDENCE_SCOPE_REVIEW.md` for the statement-by-statement scope classification and the corrected
conclusion.
