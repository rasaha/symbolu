# Varṇa Lexicon — AUTHORITATIVE (generated from lexicon_authoritative.json)

> **Generated by `gen_lexicon_md.py` — do not hand-edit; edit the JSON.** Each varṇa has two STATES
> of expression (the only fields used in a reading): `liberating_state` = sublimated, unbinding,
> expansive, dharma/mokṣa-oriented expression; `binding_state` = worldly, contractive,
> attachment-forming, bondage-producing (bīja) expression. These are NOT moral poles and are NEVER
> chosen from external labels about the referent (good/bad, useful/useless, auspicious/inauspicious)
> — the framework asks only whether an acoustic tendency BINDS consciousness or RELEASES it, and the
> phoneme chain alone decides which state is expressed. Everything under *Expanded properties* is
> source literature (P.R. Sarkar, Varṇa Vijñāna) as **interpretive metadata**, never part of the reading.

## How to write each letter (retroflex vs dental)
Retroflex **Ṭa-varga** (ṭ ṭh ḍ ḍh ṇ) vs dental **ta-varga** (t th d dh n) are different sounds with
different vṛttis. Write retroflex via diacritics (`ṭ ḍ ṇ ṣ`) or ITRANS capitals (`T D N Sh`); English
g2p routes by sound (stops t/d/n → retroflex Ṭa/Ḍa/Ṇa; dental fricatives th → dental ta/da).

## Expanded properties (source literature — interpretive metadata, NOT scoring)

### Ka  (क)
- **vṛtti:** Āśā — hope / forward-seeking desire  _(abhiipśātmaka)_
- **elemental:** flowing water (ka = water; va = water in general)
- **acoustic roots:** Kārya Brahma (the expressed/manifested universe; controller of the living world); creation / expression; Saṃvṛtti Bodhicitta (Buddhist name for the created world)
- **etymology:** kae + ḍa = 'that which produces sound'; the roar of rivers/springs inspired the hope of survival
- **mantra:** kriiṃ = ka (Kārya Brahma) + ra (luminous factor); bīja of Kālī / Bhrāmarī Tārā
- **semantic extensions:** kac = glossy; ka / keśa = hair grown on the head; keśa = ka + īśa = hair, also Nārāyaṇa; kāpālika = 'kaṃ saṃvṛtti bodhicittaṃ pālayati' = one who serves the living & non-living world
- **deity links:** Śiva (Vyomakeśa / Khakuntala — hair toward the sky; Dhurjaṭī); Nārāyaṇa (via keśa)
- **tags:** hope, manifestation, creation, expression, flowing_water, survival_signal, sound, hair, gloss, karya_brahma
- _source:_ "Ka is the acoustic root of the abhiipśātmaka áshá vrtti. It is also the acoustic root of Kárya Brahma [the expressed universe]. … Ka (kae + d́a) etymologically means 'that which produces sound'. It also means 'water'."

### Kha  (ख)
- **vṛtti:** Cintā — worry / impersonal, objective thought  _(vishuddha saṃvedanātmaka)_
- **acoustic roots:** the crude aspects of heaven (the transcendent sphere of heaven is kṣa)
- **note:** kha means 'sky' but is NOT the acoustic root of sky (that is ha); ka + ha = kha, so kha follows ka
- **tags:** worry, impersonal_thought, crude_heaven
- _source:_ "The acoustic root of áshá vrtti is ka, and that of cintá vrtti [the propensity of worry] is kha. … Impersonal thoughts are symbolized by the acoustic root kha."

### Ga  (ग)
- **vṛtti:** Ceṣṭā — effort / striving to arouse dormant potential
- **note:** the main cause of mundane development and spiritual elevation; acts in physical, psychic and spiritual spheres
- **tags:** effort, striving, development, elevation
- _source:_ "The effort made to arouse one's dormant potentiality is called ceśt́á. … Ga, being the acoustic root of ceśt́á vrtti, plays an important role in the physical, psychic and spiritual spheres."

### Gha  (घ)
- **vṛtti:** Mamatā — love / attachment (bound by time, space, individuality)
- **imagery:** the mother who sacrifices for her child yet slices kai fish without feeling; the cow who suckles then later kicks her calf — mamatā limited by relative factors; only humans can transcend it
- **tags:** attachment, love, mine-ness, relativity
- _source:_ "Mamatá, the vrtti of love and attachment …, is related to time, space and individuality. … Gha is the acoustic root of mamatá vrtti."

### Ṅa  (ङ)
- **vṛtti:** Dambha — vanity
- **history:** Vashiṣṭha is said to have learned uṋa (and the Tārā cult of Vāmācāra Tantra) from China; uṋa is used across Indo-Chinese languages
- **tags:** vanity, pride
- _source:_ "Uṋa is the acoustic root of dambha vrtti [the propensity of vanity]."

### Ca  (च)
- **vṛtti:** Viveka — conscience / discrimination
- **tags:** conscience, discrimination
- _source:_ "Ca is the acoustic root of viveka [conscience]."

### Cha  (छ)
- **vṛtti:** Vikalatā — nervous breakdown
- **note:** a mind that had been functioning properly starts malfunctioning or stops altogether
- **tags:** nervous_breakdown, collapse
- _source:_ "Cha is the acoustic root of vikalatáh vrtti [nervous breakdown]."

### Ja  (ज)
- **vṛtti:** Ahaṃkāra — ego / inflated I-feeling
- **imagery:** Aurangzeb: 'Since I was there, I controlled the situation … had I not been there the world would have met its destruction' — an expression of ahaṃkāra
- **tags:** ego, I-feeling, inflation
- _source:_ "Ja is the acoustic root of ahaḿkára vrtti (ego). The ego becomes inflated when one allows one's 'I' feeling to take a predominant role."

### Jha  (झ)
- **vṛtti:** Lolupatā / Lobha / Lolatā — greed / avarice
- **etymology:** Bengali nolá (greedy fascination of a cat/dog) derives from lola / lolatā
- **tags:** greed, avarice, craving_fascination
- _source:_ "Jha is the acoustic root of lolupatá, lobha [greed] and lolatá [avarice] vrttis."

### Ña  (ञ)
- **vṛtti:** Kapaṭatā — hypocrisy
- **note:** Sanskrit pāṣaṇḍa / Hindi pākhaṇḍī = hypocrite; three forms: (1) cheating to serve one's purpose, (2) dominating to hide one's ignorance, (3) condemning sins one secretly commits
- **tags:** hypocrisy, deceit
- _source:_ "Ina is the acoustic root of kapat́atá vrtti [hypocrisy]."

### Ṭa  (ट)
- **vṛtti:** Vitarka — overstating one's case (garrulousness + bad temper)
- **note:** NOT mere debating, and NOT kaṣāya (harsh speech to hurt); its opposite is pramita vāk (balanced, only-relevant speech). Howrah-station example.
- **tags:** overstatement, garrulousness, bad_temper
- _source:_ "T́a is the acoustic root of vitarka vrtti [overstating one's case]. … Vitarka is a combination of a bad temper and garrulousness."

### Ṭha  (ठ)

- **etymology:** N. India paschāttāpa; anu / paschāt = 'later/after', tāpa = 'heat'

### Ḍa  (ड)

### Ḍha  (ढ)

### Ṇa  (ण)

### Ta  (त)
- **vṛtti:** Jāḍya / Nidrā — staticity, long/deep sleep, intellectual dullness, spiritual inertness
- **etymology:** Tantra = 'taṃ jāḍyāt tārayet' (liberates from staticity); root tan = 'to expand' (taṃ vistāreṇa tārayet)
- **tags:** inertia, sleep, dullness, staticity
- _source:_ "Ta is the acoustic root of staticity, long sleep and deep sleep. … That which brings about the cessation of dullness and staticity is called Tantra."

### Tha  (थ)

### Da  (द)
- **vṛtti:** Krodha / Karkaśatā — peevishness / irritability
- **note:** speak nicely to a peevish person and they react adversely; speak harshly and they take it calmly
- **tags:** peevishness, irritability
- _source:_ "Da is the acoustic root of peevishness."

### Dha  (ध)
- **vṛtti:** Tṛṣṇā — thirst for acquisition (wealth, name, fame, power, prestige)
- **note:** not thirst for water; cure = divert all thought toward Parama Puruṣa
- **tags:** craving, acquisition, ambition
- _source:_ "Dha is the acoustic root of thirst for acquisition. This limitless craving … is called trśńa in Sanskrit."

### Na  (न)
- **vṛtti:** Moha — blind attachment / infatuation
- **note:** four types: deśagata (geo-sentiment), kālagata (time), idea-bound, ādhāragata (object); cure = ideation of indifference + Parama Puruṣa
- **tags:** blind_attachment, infatuation, delusion
- _source:_ "Na is the acoustic root of moha vrtti [blind attachment or infatuation]."

### Pa  (प)
- **vṛtti:** Ghṛṇā — hatred / revulsion (a fetter / pāśa)
- **note:** one of 8 pāśas (ghṛṇā, śaṅkā, bhaya, lajjā, jugupsā, kula, śīla, māna); mainly tied to the moha ripu; root pat/patati = downward fall vs ūrdhva-gati / anurakti (devotion). The 6 ripus: kāma, krodha, lobha, mada, moha, mātsarya.
- **tags:** hatred, revulsion, fetter, downfall
- _source:_ "Pa is the acoustic root of ghrńá vrtti [the propensity of hatred or revulsion]. … Pa is the acoustic root of the fetter of hatred."

### Pha  (फ)
- **vṛtti:** Bhaya — fear
- **note:** generally born of more than one factor, but mainly from the moha ripu
- **tags:** fear
- _source:_ "Pha is the acoustic root of bhaya vrtti [the propensity of fear]."

### Ba  (ब)
- **vṛtti:** Avajñā — indifference (neglecting something of value)
- **note:** distinguished from upekṣā (ignoring the truly unacceptable); related to avahelā; maitrī–karuṇā–muditā–upekṣā verse on the four attitudes
- **tags:** indifference, neglect
- _source:_ "Ba is the acoustic root of avajiṋá vrtti [indifference]. … when one neglects something which may actually have some value, that is called avajiṋá."

### Bha  (भ)
- **vṛtti:** Mūrcchā — deluded obsession (loss of common sense under a ripu's spell)
- **note:** NOT senselessness; cure = pratyāhāra yoga, or kīrtana / devotional song
- **tags:** deluded_obsession, hypnotic_spell
- _source:_ "Bha is the acoustic root of the múrcchá vrtti. … it means to lose one's common sense under the hypnotic spell of a particular ripu."

### Ma  (म)

- **tags:** annihilation, indulgence, latitude
- _source:_ "Ma is the acoustic root of prańásha [annihilation]. It is also the acoustic root of prashraya vrtti – giving latitude [indulgence]."

### Ya  (य)
- **vṛtti:** Aviśvāsa — lack of confidence (in oneself and others)
- **elemental:** constant movement (like the movement of air)
- **note:** such people say to the end 'Shall I be able to do it?' and can accomplish nothing great
- **tags:** lack_of_confidence, movement, air
- _source:_ "Ya is the acoustic root of avishvása vrtti [lack of confidence], and is also the acoustic root of constant movement (like the movement of air)."

### Ra  (र)
- **vṛtti:** Agnitattva / Prāṇaśakti (vitality) + Sarvanāśa (annihilation-thought) — vitality / fire; and the defeatist thought 'everything is gone, I am undone'
- **elemental:** agnitattva (fire); the monosyllable 'ra' means fire
- **note:** sarvanāśa cured by guru mantra auto-suggestion 'Parama Puruṣa is mine / I am destined to win'
- **mantra:** Raṃ bījaṃ shikhinaṃ dhyāyet, trikoṇam-aruṇaprabham (meditate on the bīja Raṃ, triangular, fire-red)
- **tags:** vitality, fire, agnitattva, annihilation_thought, defeat, luminous_factor
- _source:_ "Ra is the acoustic root of agnitattva or práńashakti – vitality. … It is also the acoustic root of sarvanásha [the thought of annihilation]. … the monosyllabic word ra means 'fire'."

### La  (ल)
- **vṛtti:** Krūratā — cruelty (countered by compassion)
- **elemental:** kṣititattva (the solid factor)
- **mantra:** Laṃ bījaṃ dharaṇīṃ dhyāyet, caturāsrāṃ supītābhām (meditate on bīja Laṃ, square, golden — the earth factor)
- **tags:** cruelty, solid_factor, earth
- _source:_ "La is the acoustic root of kruratá vrtti [cruelty]. … La is also the acoustic root of kśititattva, the solid factor."

### Va  (व)
- **vṛtti:** Dharma — ensconcement in one's original stance; movement toward Parama Puruṣa
- **elemental:** jalatattva (the liquid factor — any liquid, not only water)
- **note:** Dhriyate dharma ityāhuḥ — 'dharma is that which sustains'; humanity cannot sprout except in the soil of dharma
- **deity links:** Varuṇa Deva (the mythological rain-god)
- **tags:** dharma, original_stance, liquid_factor, water, sustaining
- _source:_ "Va is the acoustic root of dharma. … Va is also the acoustic root of jalatattva [the liquid factor], and the acoustic root of the rain-god Varuńa Deva."

### Śa  (श)
- **vṛtti:** Rajoguṇa + Artha — the mutative principle; and artha (psychic longing — temporary cessation of worldly wants)
- **note:** shra = mutative principle + vitality (ra); shra + ṅīṣ = shrii (hence 'Śrī' before a name, a blessing on dynamism)
- **tags:** rajoguna, mutative, artha, dynamism
- _source:_ "Sha is the acoustic root of rajoguńa [the mutative principle]. It is also the acoustic root of artha [psychic longing]."

### Ṣa  (ष)
- **vṛtti:** Tamoguṇa + Kāma — the static principle; and kāma (all physical/worldly desires — wealth, fame, position)
- **note:** one of the four vargas (dharma=va, artha=sha, kāma=ṣa, mokṣa=sa)
- **tags:** tamoguna, static, kama, worldly_desire
- _source:_ "Śa is the acoustic root of tamoguńa [the static principle], and is also the acoustic root of all kinds of worldly desires … káma."

### Sa  (स)
- **vṛtti:** Mokṣa + Sattvaguṇa — liberation (unqualified); and the sentient principle
- **note:** completes the four vargas: va=dharma, sha=artha, ṣa=kāma, sa=mokṣa; and the three guṇas: sha=rajas, ṣa=tamas, sa=sattva
- **tags:** moksa, liberation, sattvaguna, sentient
- _source:_ "Sa is the acoustic root of mokśa [salvation, unqualified liberation]. … sa is the acoustic root of sattvaguńa [the sentient principle]."

### Ha  (ह)
- **vṛtti:** Parā-vidyā — intuitional science; ethereal factor, daytime, the sun, svarloka
- **note:** opposite of ṭha (night, moon, bhūvarloka, kāmamaya kośa); ha + ao = hao (Śiva in tāṇḍava dance); Śiva as preceptor = aeṃ (also bīja of the guru and of Sarasvatī)
- **tags:** ether, day, sun, svarloka, para_vidya, intuition
- _source:_ "Ha is the acoustic root of the ethereal factor, of daytime, of the sun, of svarloka, and of parávidyá [intuitional science]."

### Kṣa  (क्ष)
- **vṛtti:** Aparā-vidyā — mundane knowledge / material science
- **note:** also the acoustic root of the transcendent sphere of heaven (vs kha = crude heaven)
- **tags:** mundane_knowledge, material_science
- _source:_ "Kśa is the acoustic root of mundane knowledge, and is also the acoustic root of material science."
