# Is "acoustic atmosphere / mental weather" a coherent scientific object?

> Conceptual evaluation (implementation ignored, prior experiments bracketed). Question: is a word's
> "latent environment / mental weather / acoustic atmosphere" a coherent object distinct from meaning —
> and if so, what must it look like? **Answer: YES as a real object — but only when built from the
> ACOUSTIC-AFFECTIVE substrate (the chord analogy), where it is a known, measurable thing (sound
> symbolism / affective iconicity). It COLLAPSES the moment it is built from the varṇa GLOSSES, which are
> semantic labels. The concept survives; the gloss instantiation does not.**

## The decisive split (everything hinges on the substrate)

"Atmosphere" can be built from two different substrates:

- **(A) Acoustic-affective features** — the felt qualities of the *sound itself*: sonority, vowel
  height/backness, sharpness, brightness, tempo (the word's "music"). A chord's atmosphere lives here.
  **This is a coherent, real, measurable object, distinct from dictionary meaning.**
- **(B) Varṇa propensity glosses** — "Hope, Cruelty, Envy, agnitattva…" concatenated. **Correction (a
  reader rightly pushed back):** these are *not* dry dictionary tokens. They denote **felt and energetic
  qualities** — vṛttis (mental modifications: envy, hope, anger are *cittavṛttis*, actual felt states)
  and tattvas (elemental principles: *agnitattva* = the fire-principle, an energetic quality, not an
  emotion and not a definition). So a varṇa profile is best read as a sequence of **affective/energetic
  qualia**, i.e. it already lives in *atmosphere space*, not in dictionary-meaning space. My earlier
  "semantic object by construction" was wrong on the category. **What stays open is different:** these
  qualities are **asserted** to attach to these sounds by the lexicon; the chord-substrate (A) qualities
  are **measured** from the form. *Measured vs asserted attachment* — not *meaning vs feeling* — is the
  real gap, and it is exactly what the scramble tests (which is indifferent to whether the labels denote
  feelings or definitions).

The proposal mixes these: it invokes (A)'s intuition (the chord) but computes (B)'s object (the glosses).
That mix is exactly where the confusion has lived.

## Answers to the four questions

**1. Different from meaning, or collapses into semantics?**
Partially different — with a precise caveat. Atmosphere built from substrate (A) is distinct from
*denotational/dictionary* meaning (a chord doesn't denote). **But it is not meaning-free: it is
*affective/connotative* meaning** — the valence/arousal/iconic channel, which linguistics and psychology
treat as a real dimension of semantics (Osgood's semantic differential; Russell's circumplex; sound
symbolism). So it does **not** collapse into *lexical* meaning, but it is itself a *kind* of meaning
(affective), not an object outside semantics entirely. Built from substrate (B) it collapses immediately
and fully into semantics, because the glosses are denotational labels.

**2. Rigorous, independently measurable definition (substrate A).**
Define the atmosphere of a word-form as its coordinate in a validated **affective / sound-symbolic
space** — e.g. ⟨valence, arousal, dominance⟩ and/or ⟨sharp–soft, bright–dark, large–small, active–still⟩.
Operationalize by eliciting **human ratings of the FORM with denotation removed**: present the sound as a
nonce word, or to listeners who don't know the language, or audio-only, and collect scale ratings. Two
researchers measure independently → check inter-rater reliability + cross-lab replication. This is a
standard, replicable paradigm (Sapir 1929; Köhler; Blasi et al. 2016). **Removing denotation is the
crux** — otherwise ratings are contaminated by what the word means.

**3. Can it exist without being "just another semantic embedding"?**
Yes, conditionally. A distributional embedding (word2vec/LLM) encodes meaning from *textual context*; the
atmosphere is elicited from *sound-form*. They are different sources. To prove the atmosphere is not
merely the embedding, you must show it carries variance **orthogonal to** (beyond) the semantic embedding
— i.e. form-driven affect not predictable from meaning. That residual is **real but small** (this is
exactly what Blasi measured: sound predicts meaning slightly beyond relatedness). So: a genuine distinct
object exists, but empirically it is a *small orthogonal component*, not a large independent space.

**4. If it collapses, where exactly?**
Two precise collapse points:
- **Substrate collapse:** representing the atmosphere with the **varṇa glosses** (semantic labels) makes
  it a semantic object instantly — there is nothing acoustic left, only emotion-nouns. The "atmosphere"
  becomes a list of meanings.
- **Mapping collapse:** asking whether the **specific** sound→gloss assignment captures the atmosphere
  (vs a scrambled assignment) is the sound→(affective)meaning hypothesis — and the relabeling-invariance
  theorem / six nulls apply: the specific mapping is unobservable unless you use the glosses' meanings,
  which is the falsified channel. So the *varṇa* atmosphere collapses; the *acoustic-feature* atmosphere
  does not (it has no gloss to scramble — its control is random *phonetic-feature* assignment).

## The chord analogy, used precisely

The analogy is correct *and* it argues against the gloss method. A chord's atmosphere is real and
measurable — **and it comes directly from the acoustics** (intervals, consonance/dissonance,
register), not from a lookup table mapping each note-name to an emotion word. The word-analog is the
affective quality of the **phonemes themselves**, measured directly — not a fixed sound→gloss dictionary
concatenated into a profile. So the chord supports substrate (A) and undercuts substrate (B).

## What the object must look like (the "yes" construction)

For "acoustic atmosphere" to be a coherent scientific object you can later test:
1. **Substrate = phonetic/acoustic features**, never gloss labels.
2. **Codomain = a validated affective/iconic space** (valence–arousal–dominance, or sound-symbolic axes).
3. **Measurement = human ratings of FORM with denotation controlled** (nonce words / naïve listeners /
   audio), inter-subjectively reliable and cross-lab replicable.
4. **Distinctness test = orthogonal variance** beyond a distributional semantic embedding (Blasi-style
   phylogenetic/areal/meaning controls), with the control being a **random phonetic-feature** baseline,
   not a scrambled-gloss one.
5. The varṇa lexicon's only honest role: a **cultural vocabulary for naming regions** of that
   acoustic-affective space — descriptive skin over a measurable acoustic core, not the core itself.

## Bottom line

**The concept is coherent and real — it is essentially affective phonetic iconicity / the connotative
channel, which exists and is measurable, and is distinct from *dictionary* meaning (though it is a
sub-kind of affective meaning, not meaning-free).** The varṇa-gloss instantiation is **not** that object:
it renders the atmosphere in semantic labels and pins it to a specific sound→gloss map, which is where it
collapses into the already-falsified sound→meaning channel. Keep the concept; change the substrate from
glosses to measured sound-affect.
